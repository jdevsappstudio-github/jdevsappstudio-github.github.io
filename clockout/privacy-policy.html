Privacy Policy

Desk Rush: Office Survivor · Last updated 24 November 2026

Desk Rush: Office Survivor, formerly called Clock Out ("Desk Rush", "the game"), is an Android game by JDevsAppStudio ("we", "us"). This page explains what the game collects, why, who receives it, and how you can control it.

In short

  • There's no account with us, and we run no servers. Your career is saved on your phone.
  • If you sign in to Google Play Games, Google also keeps a copy of your career in your Google account.
  • Ads, gameplay analytics and crash reports are handled by the companies listed below, using random IDs, never your name or email.
  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, none of these start unless you agree. You can change your answer any time in Settings.
  • We don't sell your data.

Your game save

Your career (stages, stars, pay, upgrades, desk, skins and settings) is stored on your phone, in the game's own storage, with a backup copy in the app's private files. It isn't sent to us.

If Android backup is turned on for your phone, Android may include that backup copy in your device backup in your Google account, so the game can restore it on a new phone. This is controlled by your phone's backup settings, not by the game.

Google Play Games (optional)

You can play without signing in. If you sign in to Google Play Games (in Settings, or when the game offers it after your first promotion), or if your phone already has a Play Games profile, the game uses Google Play Games Saved Games to keep a copy of your career in your Google account, so it can come back on another phone.

  • Google stores this copy in a private area of your Google account that only Desk Rush can use. It goes from your phone to Google. We never receive a copy.
  • Google gives the game your Play Games player ID so it can find your save. The game doesn't receive your email address.
  • Google's own privacy policy applies to your Play Games profile.

Ads

The game shows rewarded ads only, and only when you choose to watch one: to revive after burning out, or to double your pay after a stage. There are no banner ads and no forced ads.

Ads are served through Unity LevelPlay, an ad mediation service, by the ad networks Unity Ads and ironSource Ads (both part of Unity). To show, measure and protect ads against fraud, they may collect your phone's advertising ID, IP address, device information (such as model, Android version and language) and how you interact with ads. They may use this to show ads based on your interests.

  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (on most phones: Settings > Google > Ads, or Settings > Privacy > Ads).
  • The ad libraries also use Android's ad services (the Privacy Sandbox), which you can manage in your phone's privacy settings.
  • With Desk Rush Pro, the game never shows you ads.

Gameplay analytics

We use PostHog (servers in the United States) to learn which stages are too hard, where new players get stuck, and how the game's economy is working. The game sends a small set of events, such as: the app was opened, a stage started or ended (with the result, time, stars and pay), a revive was used, an ad was watched or wasn't available, the Pro screen was opened or Pro was bought, and in-game pay was earned or spent.

Each event comes with your difficulty setting, the app version, basic device information (such as phone model, Android version, language and time zone) and a random ID made by the app. PostHog is set to discard IP addresses. Events never include your name, email or Google account.

Crash reports

We use Firebase Crashlytics by Google to find and fix crashes and errors. When the game crashes or hits an error, it sends a report with technical details about what went wrong, your phone model, Android version, app version and a random Crashlytics installation ID. The game doesn't use Firebase Analytics.

Purchases

Desk Rush Pro is a one-time purchase. Google Play handles the payment, and we never see your payment details. We use RevenueCat to confirm and remember that you own Pro and to restore it on a new phone. RevenueCat receives a random app user ID and your purchase records from Google Play, not your name or email.

Your choices

  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland: the game asks the first time you open it. If you accept, ads, analytics and crash reports start. If you decline, none of them start. You can still revive, by spending in-game pay instead of watching an ad. You can change your answer any time in Settings > Ad choices. Withdrawing stops these services from then on and deletes crash reports that haven't been sent yet. The game works out your region once, from your SIM or mobile network country (or your phone's language if neither is available), and remembers it.
  • In the United States: Settings > Do not sell or share my data tells the ad networks not to sell or share your data for personalized ads.
  • Everywhere: you can reset your advertising ID, buy Pro to stop ads, play without signing in to Play Games, and uninstall the game at any time.

Permissions

The game asks for no permissions while you play, and it can't read your location, contacts, photos, files, camera or microphone. Its app listing includes:

  • Internet and network state: for ads, purchases, cloud save, analytics and crash reports, and to tell you when you're offline.
  • Advertising ID and Android ad services: used by the ad networks, as described above.
  • Google Play billing: to buy Pro.
  • Keep awake, run at startup and foreground service: added by Google's and the ad networks' libraries to finish short background tasks, such as sending a report that was queued while you were offline.

Sharing and selling

We don't sell your data. Data goes only to the service providers named on this page, for the purposes described here. Ad networks may use data for personalized ads, which some US state laws call "selling" or "sharing". Use the switch in Settings to opt out.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • On your phone: uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes your save and settings from the phone.
  • Google Play Games: to delete your cloud save, open the Google Play Games app, go to Settings, and use the option to delete your Play Games account and data. There you can delete Desk Rush's data on its own, or your whole profile.
  • Analytics, crash reports, ads and purchases: these are linked to random IDs, not to you by name, and each provider keeps them only as long as its retention settings allow. Because there's no account, we usually can't tell which data is yours. If you'd like us to look into deleting data, email us and we'll help where we can. You can also contact the providers directly.

Security

Data sent by the game and its providers is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Your save on the phone is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read.

Children

Desk Rush is made for a general audience aged 13 and over, and isn't directed to children under 13. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game and data was collected, contact us and we'll help remove it.

Service providers

Their own privacy policies apply to the data described above:

  • PostHog: PostHog privacy policy
  • Firebase Crashlytics: Firebase privacy and security
  • Google Play, Google Play Games and Google Play billing: Google privacy policy
  • RevenueCat: RevenueCat privacy policy
